Across two semesters of design-based service learning, we studied human-centered design and social innovation while developing real-world solutions to difficult community challenges. The program closes with an intensive period in Spain built around design collaboration, rapid prototyping, field testing, and cultural immersion. For me, it was the first time I could see how engineering decisions shape the daily life of an entire city." - title: "Listening Before Designing" description: "The most formative work happened away from any classroom. Working alongside our community partner, we went door to door in residential neighborhoods assessing the needs of elderly residents. We asked about their daily routines, their struggles, and the support they lacked. That is human-centered design in its purest form. You cannot engineer a solution for a community you have not listened to. The experience changed how I approach engineering problems. Requirements gathering starts with people, not with a CAD model." - title: "Barcelona as a Living Laboratory" description: "Barcelona treats its urban infrastructure as a design problem, and the smart city tour made that clear. We examined how the city layers modern systems onto centuries-old streets, and how decisions about transit, energy, and public space directly affect quality of life. We also visited a social garden and saw how a shared green space builds community cohesion and resident wellbeing. Those are engineering outcomes that never appear on a drawing, but they are the reason the work matters." - title: "The Engineering Connection" description: "The Hive is also where I practice engineering in public. Explaining a technical concept to someone with zero technical background is the same discipline as writing clear engineering documentation. Keeping the 3D printers running taught me preventive maintenance, root cause troubleshooting, and the patience to iterate on settings until results are repeatable. Those habits transfer directly to mechanical design, fabrication, and plant operations." - title: "Cultural Immersion" description: "The technical work was only half of the experience. I learned flamenco, danced to traditional music, and visited historical sites, and spent time talking with locals about their city. Engineering in a global context requires more than translated specifications. It requires genuine curiosity about and respect for the people who live with the results of your work. I left Spain with a clearer understanding of how culture shapes infrastructure, and how infrastructure shapes culture." - title: "Where This Leads" description: "The Spain program confirmed the direction of my career. Watching Barcelona balance its history with modern urban systems is what solidified my goal of pursuing graduate study in Urban Innovation and Smart Cities at the Universitat Politècnica de Catalunya. It also started a habit I intend to keep. This December I will join a similar EPICS intensive program in Indonesia to keep studying how different communities approach the same fundamental challenges. Engineering is a global practice, and I intend to train for it globally." ---
